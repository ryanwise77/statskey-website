The Gut Check

Seven types. Mixed episodes. Fifteen seconds.

The recorder is three tabs deep and zero judgment wide. Most days it's two taps; on the days that matter, it captures what a gastroenterologist would actually ask about.

Type

The Bristol scale, color-coded and named — and when one movement changes form mid-episode, add phases: each gets its own type and portion, from Trace to All.

Timing

Quick-select capsules (1–20 min), an exact minutes:seconds entry, or a live stopwatch you start when you sit down.

Details

Size, color, urgency, control, passage, cleanup, red flags, a private photo, feel tags, and notes — every one optional, every one structured.

    Private by design

    Your record. Your face to unlock it.

    The Bowel Record Lock masks every bowel row in the app — timeline, history, search — behind a 4–8 digit passcode with Face ID or Touch ID. Unlock once; it relocks the moment the app leaves the foreground.

    Private photos go further: owner-only cloud storage, never shown on the timeline, and blurred until you deliberately tap Reveal. Wellness records never appear to friends, and substance records are excluded from Intelligence, reports, and sharing by default.

    Honest fine print, straight from the app: it is an in-app viewing lock, not end-to-end encryption — and a forgotten passcode requires re-confirming your sign-in to reset.

    The gut–brain axis

    Stress talks to your gut. Read the transcript.

    The Gut-Brain Check-In takes fifteen seconds: mood, a 0–10 stress read, and optional gut symptoms. Record stress of 6 or higher alongside a symptom and StatsKey quietly tags stress as a trigger.

    Once ten days carry both a stress reading and a gut record, the Patterns card computes a real correlation across your days — and tells you the honest version: a link, an inverse link, or "no clear pattern yet."

    Pearson correlation on paired days only · requires ≥10 paired days and |r| ≥ 0.20 before claiming anything · high-stress vs calm-day contrast needs ≥4 days per bucket. An association, not proof — and not medical advice.

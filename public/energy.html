The principle

Why investment comes in steps.

Uncertainty is the cost driver in every drilling and acquisition decision. The rational response is not one big bet — it is a sequence of smaller ones, each sized to the uncertainty that remains, and each capable of changing the decision that follows it.

01

Screen wide, spend little

Public records make the first pass nearly free. A ranked list narrows thousands of candidates to a testable few — before any capital is at risk.

02

Test where the answer is known

Locked forecasts are scored against wells with recorded outcomes. A cheap test that can change an expensive decision is the best money in the program.

03

Commit as evidence compounds

Capital steps up only when the previous stage's forecasts held. Each tier of spend is justified by the uncertainty the last tier removed — never by the promise of the next.

Information is only worth buying when it can change a decision — so the cheap information comes first, and the size of every bet follows the strength of the record, not the strength of the story.
The price

Priced the way this industry pays its partners.

Oil and gas has never paid the people who de-risk capital by the hour. The prospect generator keeps an override. The underwriter takes points on the deal. The promoter is paid in carry. StatsKey prices inside that tradition — cash that covers the work, interest that carries the conviction — because a fee small enough to ignore is evidence the model is too.

The economics underneath are owned, not rented: corpus, model, and machines are built once, run around the clock on dedicated hardware, and reused by every engagement that follows — costs that do not move when cloud and GPU prices do.

No unpaid pilots. No day rates.

Screening is priced per wellbore, decisions per AFE, deals per deal size. The backtest is paid work, because it is the product. Hourly engagement converts an underwriter into a contractor; we decline it.

Your data stays yours

Operator data is used only to deliver that operator's engagement. It trains nothing used for anyone else — any broader use requires a separate, priced, written data-rights agreement. Exclusivity is never implied; where it makes sense, basin- or area-level exclusivity is a separately priced term.

Every engagement opens the same way: a scored blind backtest on wells whose outcomes are already known — paid, because it is the product; decisive, because it is scored. The pass bar — metric, wells, and window — is agreed in writing before anything is scored. Clear it and the engagement scales; miss it and you walk away having bought exactly that answer.

The schedule is the standard rate card; scope and interests are papered per engagement under a master agreement, structured to the asset and jurisdiction. Nothing on this page is an offer of securities, legal advice, or investment advice.

The bigger number

The seven-figure value sits on the operator's side of the table.

The structure is deliberately asymmetric: our side is the schedule plus small interests. The larger arithmetic belongs to the asset owner, because the value shows up as redirected capital on wells and deals they already control.

01

Workover triage at field scale

Across a thousand legacy wellbores, surfacing ten economic workovers a year at a $250,000 average cost, each returning about twice its cost, creates roughly $2.5M of annual value on assets already owned. Our share is the fee and a small interest; the rest stays in the field.

02

Acquisition underwriting

On producing-asset deals priced in the tens of millions, data-complete diligence that moves price or catches one bad assumption is worth 1–2% of deal size — $500,000 to $1,000,000 on a single $50M transaction, recurring with deal cadence.

03

Plug-or-justify triage

Every legacy wellbore justified rather than plugged avoids a $30,000–$100,000 plugging liability. Across the hundreds of wells facing that decision in a mature field, the triage alone is a seven-figure line.

Scale is evidence-gated: the engagement opens with a paid screen at the schedule's minimum, and expands on the schedule only after the backtest clears the bar agreed before scoring — our staging principle, applied to ourselves.

The rule

Prove it before capital follows.

Lock each forecast before the well result. Compare it with the current workflow on later wells and areas the model has never seen. Use it only if it picks better wells or forecasts cost and production better after data and operating costs.

Intelligence can improve the odds. It cannot remove geological, execution, price, title, safety, or regulatory risk.

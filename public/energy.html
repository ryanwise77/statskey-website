The principle

Generalization beats interpretation.

Superior data, not cleverer features. A model trained on a representative sample of the earth's subsurface learns structure that transfers — the same way large models generalize in every other domain.

One field teaches one field; a thousand surveys teach the rock. The scale and breadth of representative data is the moat — not any single clever model.

Compute is the enabler, not the edge. Modern accelerators are simply what finally make training on a corpus this large possible.

Majors aim this at their own acreage. No one aims it at the whole public record.
The industry gap

Everyone touches the data. No one underwrites the whole record.

Upstream is one of the most data-rich industries on earth, yet the layer between raw subsurface data and the capital decision is still unclaimed. Each camp builds for its own incentive — and leaves the independent underwriting layer open.

Majors

DoingProprietary AI and supercomputing to interpret their own seismic and accelerate exploration.
Leaves openThe proof stays private — locked to their acreage, data, and balance sheet. It never becomes an independent product.

Service companies

DoingSelling tools for interpretation, field optimization, and existing-well workflows.
Leaves openThey sell software and services — not the capital allocator deciding which outside asset is mispriced.

Data vendors

DoingMonetizing seismic libraries, production databases, and deal-screening products.
Leaves openClosest to it — but still data and analytics supply, not forward-tested, rock-to-production underwriting.

AI-native explorers

DoingApplying AI to new-energy plays — geothermal, hydrogen, and minerals.
Leaves openThey prove the AI-explorer playbook; the oilfield-specific opportunity stays fragmented and underbuilt.

Academia & foundation models

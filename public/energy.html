The principle

Generalization beats interpretation.

Superior data, not cleverer features. A model trained on a representative sample of the earth's subsurface learns structure that transfers — the same way large models generalize in every other domain.

One field teaches one field; a thousand surveys teach the rock. The scale and breadth of representative data is the moat — not any single clever model.

Compute is the enabler, not the edge. Modern accelerators are simply what finally make training on a corpus this large possible.

Majors aim this at their own acreage. No one aims it at the whole public record.
The industry gap

Everyone models their own acreage. No model predicts the whole record.

The unclaimed layer is one model that does two things at once — statistically, and in basins it has never seen: predict where economic oil is most likely to be, and inform how to drill it. Today that capability is split across camps that each solve a slice for their own incentive, and no one connects it end to end on the public record.

How the model improves

Teach it what was known before drilling. Grade it on what happened after.

The model learns the way an experienced geologist reasons, but at much larger scale. For each well, it looks at the evidence available before the decision — seismic shape, nearby wells, maps, logs, geology, and completion plan — then compares its prediction with what the well actually produced. Repeating that before-and-after test across enough rock is how it learns which signals travel and which were local noise.

Step
What it means
Why it matters
Keep the test honest
Only show the model what would have been known before the well was drilled: seismic, maps, nearby logs, and basin context.
Then grade it against what happened later: production, dry-hole risk, payout, and decline. It cannot cheat by seeing the answer first.
Learn the rock, not the address
Nearby wells are a starting point, not the edge. The model looks for repeated shapes and textures in the seismic, plus log-derived rock quality, structure, pressure clues, and completion context.
A channel, carbonate buildup, fault shadow, or poor reservoir can show up in more than one county. Those patterns matter more than coordinates.
Compare like with like
A good result in one basin may be ordinary in another. The model scores each well against its own play, geology, pressure regime, and drilling era.
That lets it learn relative quality instead of confusing different kinds of reservoirs, technologies, and time periods.
Make it predict the unseen
Train on older wells or other areas, then ask it to predict newer wells and regions it never saw during training.
That is the closest test to a real capital decision: the model must work before the answer exists.
Let every well teach it
Each new well, recompletion, miss, and production history becomes another before-and-after lesson.
The corpus compounds. Over time, the model learns which visual signals were real and which merely looked persuasive.

The practical goal is not perfect prediction. It is a better ranking of choices: fewer low-probability wells funded, more high-probability intervals pursued, and downside cases that are visible before capital is spent.

How drilling improves

The odds improve when the next well is ranked against every analog the model has seen.

Today, drilling decisions usually combine seismic interpretation, nearby wells, type curves, geosteering models, completion assumptions, and expert judgment. That works, but it is still mostly local: what did nearby rock do, what does the interpreter see, and how much uncertainty can the team tolerate? The model adds a statistical layer underneath that workflow — a probability-ranked view of which rock is more likely to produce economic barrels and how drilling choices should change.

~25–40%

Exploration odds today

Commercial exploration success often lives in this range, and frontier plays can be lower. The opportunity is to move capital toward the better-ranked end of that risk curve.

60–80%

Development is safer, not solved

Known-field wells are more likely to find hydrocarbons. The harder question is economic quality: which location, interval, and completion earns the best return.

Lift

How accuracy gets measured

Compare model-ranked wells against offset wells, play averages, and type curves — then measure hit rate, payout, avoided weak wells, and whether the lift holds in new areas.

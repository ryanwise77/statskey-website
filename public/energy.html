The experiment

Trained on the Gulf Coast. Verified on a basin it never saw.

Stated like a lab protocol, because it was run like one. The holdout is a public benchmark, so any geoscientist can reproduce the test.

Instrument
2.5-D seismic context encoder, self-supervised (no human labels), 1,000,000 training steps
Training corpus
9 prepared 3-D volumes, 54 GB — Gulf Coast marine surveys (NAMSS/BOEM public archive) + Stratton, South Texas onshore
Holdout
Teapot Dome (NPR-3), Wyoming — different basin, different depositional setting, zero survey overlap with training. Held-out public benchmark.
Task
24-way forced-choice context retrieval; N = 384 trials (16 evaluation batches × 24)
Null hypothesis
No transferable structure learned → success probability 1/24 per trial (≈ 4.2%)
Result
384 of 384 correct. Embedding cosine alignment 0.965, flat across the final ten evaluations (steps 440k–640k).
P(384/384 | H0) = (1/24)384 ≈ 10−530
Correlation-discounted (Neff ≥ 30 independent regions):  p < (1/24)30 ≈ 10−41

Seismic crops from one survey are spatially correlated, so we do not claim the naive figure. Even under a severe discount — treating the 384 trials as only ~30 independent observations — the null is rejected at p < 10−40. There is no version of this arithmetic in which the result is chance.

Measuring principles

Six rules we don't break.

The failure mode of subsurface AI isn't bad models — it's good-looking numbers produced by broken measurement. These rules are how we keep our numbers honest.

R1 · INSTRUMENT DISCIPLINE

Calibrated, or not used

A model gets a stated measurement range, a blind test, and error bounds — or it does not touch a decision. Same rule as any gauge in service.

R2 · NO LEAKAGE

Phase-tagged evidence

Every feature is tagged pre-drill, during-drill, or post-drill. A pre-drill prediction can only consume pre-drill evidence — enforced by the schema, not by good intentions.

R3 · BLOCKED VALIDATION

No random splits

Nearby wells and adjacent crops correlate; random cross-validation inflates skill. We hold out whole surveys and whole basins, and report the in-domain vs out-of-domain gap.

R4 · CALIBRATION FIRST

40% must mean 40%

Accuracy is secondary to calibration: when we say 40%, it must happen about 40% of the time. Reliability curves and P10–P90 coverage are the reported product.

R5 · ARCHIVE BEFORE OUTCOME

No retroactive grading

Every prediction is frozen — feature snapshot, model version, timestamp — before the outcome exists. Scoring happens when reality arrives, not after it's known.

R6 · STATED CEILINGS

Error bars on the promise

Pre-drill outcomes carry irreducible geologic variance. We publish what the data cannot predict — a model without a stated ceiling is a sales document, not a measurement.

Honest limits

Useful gains are real. A universal discovery model is not.

Every claim on this page sits on exactly one rung of this ladder — and says so.

Measured

Cross-basin representation transfer. The encoder resolves seismic structure in a basin it never trained on, at p < 10−40. This is the verified foundation for analog search and screening.

In test

Calibrated opportunity screening. Ranked candidates with reliability-curve validation, scored against recorded outcomes on wells where the answer is already known — the current engineering milestone.

Not claimed

Reserves, production forecasts, or "AI finds oil." Representation quality is not outcome skill. We will claim commercial predictive skill when — and only when — the archived-prediction scoreboard shows it.

Why we lead with what we can't do: in a market of overclaimed AI, the vendor who states ceilings is the one whose numbers you can use. Ask any subsurface AI vendor for their held-out validation splits. We publish ours.

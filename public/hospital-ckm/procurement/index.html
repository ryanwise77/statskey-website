1Executive summary

Hospital CKM is a research-stage bedside continuous potassium monitoring concept for care settings where potassium can change faster than scheduled labs: ICU, dialysis, CRRT, DKA/insulin therapy, post-cardiac surgery, transplant induction, refeeding risk, and high-risk arrhythmia beds.

The procurement objective is not to buy a clinical device. It is to issue a bounded research purchase order for one orderable, testable first article and the supporting engineering package needed to answer three feasibility questions:

  1. Can a closed disposable flow-cell produce stable, redundant potassium signals in controlled electrolyte and dialysate-like matrices?
  2. Can onboard QC suppress misleading output during bubbles, stale flow, reference drift, temperature change, fouling, or channel disagreement?
  3. Can the workflow support a hospital-safe posture: trend surveillance, confirmatory sample trigger, audit record, and no autonomous dosing?

The package below turns the public Hospital CKM product thesis into a buyer-facing document: scope, pricing framework, component list, validation gates, schedule, assumptions, and contracting paths.

2Scope of supply

  • One (1) Hospital CKM research first article: reusable reader envelope, transparent disposable flow-cell cassette, triplicate K+ channels, dual reference, blank membrane control, temperature, flow, pressure, and bubble diagnostics.
  • Duplicate wet-path consumables: second flow-cell body, spare fittings, tubing, KCl standards, waste capture, protein/salt matrix supplies, and enough electrodes/membrane chemistry to repeat assembly and step-response tests.
  • Bench electronics stack: six same-family guarded electrometer channels, ADS124S08 24-bit multi-channel ADC, TMP117 temperature sensing, digital flow measurement, pressure-bridge acquisition, pump control, ESP32-S3 data capture, and isolated prototype power.
  • Research data-capture software for raw mV, temperature, flow, pressure, pump state, reference delta, blank channel, channel agreement, sample label, calibration state, and withheld-output reason.
  • Written bench protocol covering KCl standards, dialysate/CRRT-like matrices, drift, fouling, bubbles, stale flow, carryover, interferences, and controlled failure modes.
  • Comparator-first validation plan for later blood gas, i-STAT/POC, central lab, and dialysis/CRRT chemistry comparisons.
  • Procurement binder: BOM, assembly drawings, risk register, acceptance gates, and pilot-readiness decision memo.
This scope intentionally starts outside patient care. Patient-facing use requires additional sterility, biocompatibility, electrical safety, cybersecurity, human factors, IRB, and regulatory work.

8Validated chemistry basis

Potassium-selective potentiometry with valinomycin is a mature, clinically used measurement principle. That does not validate this flow cell. Packaging, activity-versus-concentration behavior, reference contamination, drift, calibration, matrix effects, and safe interpretation still require direct evidence.

AreaProcurement decisionValidation implication
K+ sensor chemistryMake active and matched blank solvent-polymer membranes from separately controlled components. Use premixed cocktail 99373 only as an early liquid-carrier route, not as proof of the final PVC stack.Verify slope, intercept, response time, drift, adhesion, water-layer behavior, and lot-to-lot reproducibility before matrix testing.
Clinical precedentKeep the claim tied to ion-selective electrode potentiometry, the same broad principle used by electrolyte analyzers and single-use blood cartridges.Prototype evidence must still be generated; precedent supports feasibility, not equivalence to an FDA-cleared analyzer.
Major interferencesDesign tests for ammonium first, then sodium, calcium, magnesium, lithium, pH/bicarbonate, protein, hemolysis, lipemia, icterus, and heparin effects.Ammonium is the most important K+ ISE interference to bound; sodium is high concentration but usually lower selectivity risk for valinomycin membranes.
Reference systemProcure two double-junction Ag/AgCl references with potassium-free outer bridge electrolyte, then plan independent integrated reservoirs.Reference drift and KCl leakage are central accuracy failure modes; either reference fault suppresses output.
Activity and matrixCalibrate clean KCl response first, then use standards with the same ionic background as each dialysate/CRRT matrix.ISEs respond to ion activity. A clean-water calibration cannot support a concentration claim in a high-sodium/bicarbonate matrix.
Miniaturization pathUse commercial SPEs for first data, then move to custom printed lanes with solid-contact MWCNT/PEDOT:PSS underlayers.Solid contact improves potential stability, but every custom lane needs water-layer, impedance/drift, and lot-release testing.
Flow-cell chemistry exposureKeep first clinical-adjacent work in dialysate/CRRT effluent or a membrane-separated compartment before direct blood contact.This follows the safer extracorporeal literature pattern: protect sensors from proteins/coagulation while proving continuous electrolyte tracking.

Research basis checked: Metrohm/DropSens ISE guidance, valinomycin membrane literature, CRRT in-line electrolyte monitoring, FDA i-STAT potassium ISE summaries, CLSI EP19 lifecycle guidance, and vendor datasheets for flow/pump/pressure/electronics components.

10How far from usable at scale

The measurement principle is credible enough to justify a bench first article, but the integrated architecture is not yet released or clinically validated. It must close the sensor carrier, references, hydraulic response, electronics, calibration, and failure modes before any scale claim.

13Assumptions and exclusions

  • All work is research-stage and non-clinical unless a separate clinical protocol is approved.
  • This procurement is potassium-first. Sodium, calcium, chloride, bicarbonate/CO2, creatinine, BUN/urea, lactate, glucose, pH, or cystatin C require separate chemistry selection, cross-sensitivity analysis, and validation gates before being sold as Hospital CKM outputs.
  • No diagnosis, treatment, autonomous dosing, or replacement of blood gas/central lab potassium is included.
  • Sterile packaging, biocompatibility, hemocompatibility, IEC 60601 testing, ISO 14971 risk management, IEC 62304 software lifecycle, human factors, FDA submission, and production quality-system work are excluded from the first-article package but required for the clinical path.
  • Hospital provides comparator access, clinical workflow reviewers, institutional approvals, and de-identified data access only under separate agreement.
  • Prices exclude tax, institutional overhead, IRB fees, shipping expedites, hospital integration fees, and customer-requested scope changes.

15Why this package

  • It is specific. The buyer can procure a defined first article instead of funding an open-ended concept.
  • It preserves hospital safety posture. Every claim routes through comparator confirmation and fail-closed output suppression.
  • It starts in the easiest matrix. Dialysate/CRRT/effluent-style testing proves continuous electrolyte sensing before blood-contacting designs.
  • It creates decision evidence. The deliverable is not only hardware; it is data, risk closure, and a clear go/no-go recommendation for simulation or pilot work.

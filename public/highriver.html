The focus

How predictive power scales with computing.

Your data is fixed and fully used from day one. The free variable is how hard the model is trained — computing on the public archive alongside your volumes. Each link below is measured in seismic and well work or borrowed from measured scaling studies; we say which.

The chain measured where marked · otherwise from general machine-learning scaling studies
01

Computing buys a better-read model

More computing buys a larger model, trained longer and at higher resolution, on more of the public record. Measured in seismic: along the joint model-and-data frontier, interpretation error falls as roughly compute to the −0.28 — it halves for every elevenfold increase (5-shot error 11.5% → 1.0% over about 5,600×), with no saturation seen up to 85M parameters. Parameters buy more than data per log-unit at that scale. (TGS 2D grid, 2025.)

02

Basin-targeted and higher-resolution training pays

Measured: pretraining on the basin’s own archive beat a global model by 4–8 points on average and 7–17 points on amplitude and direct-hydrocarbon-indicator tasks; 2.5D context cost 4× the computing for +3.8 points, dense 3D 14× for +0.5 (Norwegian shelf model, 2026). Pretraining beat starting from scratch by 14–24 points with only 100 labels, and doubling input resolution added 7 (SFM, Geophysics 2025). Growing from 660M to 1.8B parameters left the held-out salt benchmark unchanged (TGS).

03

A better-read model multiplies your data

Because the model has already learned the public record, each of your wells teaches it more than it would teach a fresh model — about 30% more effective data per doubling of the base model’s size (transfer scaling; borrowed exponent). That is why a backtest across your whole portfolio already works, and why the second area costs less to rank than the first.

04

Effective data lowers forecast error

Each doubling of the wells the model effectively learns from removes roughly 5–10% of the error that is still removable (borrowed data exponent). Measured in oil: a model that went from about 360 to 900 wells lost a 20% forecast bias entirely; models beat planning type curves 15% to 20% error on held-out wells; models trained on a basin’s other counties cut error 30–48% in thin counties.

05

The composite, honestly

Every tenfold of pretraining computing removes about 2–10% of the error still removable; out-of-basin transfer damps it further. The large, measured gain is information — seismic and well data in one model: forecasts 15–35% better than decline methods; seismic-derived features lifting explained variance from about 0.4 to 0.85–0.89 where wells sit inside 3D coverage; calibrated intervals 14% narrower at the same coverage. Computing on top is a slow, steady power law — and we stop when it flattens.

Grades: measured, model-inferred, or physically principled but not yet measured.

01

Where to land in the zone

Physics: the target’s thickness and porosity come from seismic inversion (impedance answers to porosity and lithology); its structure and dip from the reflectors (stay in zone, avoid faults and water); where fractures will open from the elastic properties (compressional and shear velocity and density give Young’s modulus and Poisson’s ratio — stress and brittleness). Statistics: offsets with known landings and outcomes give the size of the landing effect — in one basin study, top-half landings made about +5% and bottom-half about −10% of three-year oil (model-inferred, no confidence intervals). The landing method transfers to stacked benches like the Marmaton–Cleveland interval, but no Marmaton-specific model exists yet — the coefficients must be fit on local wells, not imported. One vendor case study reports seismic-guided development of thin benches worth about $1.5M NPV per well against the operator’s own pre-survey wells; it is a vendor figure, to be tested on 10–20 of your wells before it is believed. Our role: a map of expected rock quality along the planned path with ranges, and the expected production difference between candidate landings. The engineer chooses the target.

02

How to complete it

Physics: fracture geometry follows pumped volume and the stress it meets; productivity scales with conductive fracture area until fractures interfere, so returns diminish. Measured: grouping stages by similar stress from mechanical-property logs raised cluster efficiency from 64% to 82% in one Eagle Ford program; a 12,000-well Bakken study found the proppant and fluid optimum differs by productivity area, so there is no single “more is better” curve; and a Bakken learning study found operators had captured about two-thirds (67%) of the profit available from their input choices — real, measured headroom. Statistics: hundreds of offsets with completion records give the marginal-return curve for intensity in this rock, with its uncertainty. Our role: the curve and where its knee is, for this rock. The engineer sets the design.

03

How close to the next well

Physics: an existing well has drawn down the pressure around it (material balance), so a child well placed inside that drainage recovers less; the penalty grows with proximity and time. Statistics: measured — child wells run up to about 40% below unbounded parents, and the penalty depends on completion size (more than 30% for large jobs at tight spacing, about 10% for small ones), so spacing and intensity cannot be chosen separately; the held-out Delaware study only reached 15% error against 20% once spacing was expressed correctly. Our role: the expected penalty for each candidate spacing and completion, with a range.

04

Steering while drilling

Physics: the seismic model predicts the layer geometry ahead of the bit; logging-while-drilling then corrects it. Statistics: that is a prior from seismic and offsets and a posterior from the new measurements — Bayesian updating — and re-deciding as information arrives is worth more than deciding once (a 2026 portfolio study: +32% expected value over a one-shot plan). Our role: the prior and the update rule — a live target surface with uncertainty — alongside the directional team. Frontier: real-time inversion for geosteering is commercial (a five-year Petrobras deployment was signed in 2025) but has published no precision or production metrics; Bayesian and reinforcement-learning well-placement methods are academic; and no published work yet feeds seismic-model representations into steering or completion models — we say so, and we score it rather than assume it.

05

West Panhandle shallow gas: a century-old pressure sink, so deliverability is set by drawdown against line pressure — compression and the gathering you own are a first-order lever, and the Red Cave is tight, so fracture size is the drilling lever (upsized fracs roughly doubled recovery in the play). Marmaton horizontals: landing, lateral length, and completion intensity, with hundreds of public offsets to learn from — and a thin published literature for this basin, which is why the model is fit on local wells rather than borrowed from the Permian. The Strawn CO2 flood: pattern and injection physics — sweep and CO2 utilization — where our role is smaller and the flood stays with your reservoir engineers.

06

What the well will make

Statistics: a model that has learned the record forecasts a planned well with 15% error against 20% for planning type curves on held-out wells, and gives a range that covers what it claims. Our role: that range, its measured reliability, and the ranking of the alternatives in front of you. The decision, and the well, are yours.

Evidence grades: forecast error and spacing effects are measured on held-out wells; completion diminishing returns and the 60%-of-available-profit result are measured; the landing effect is model-inferred; seismic-derived geomechanics for stage placement and machine-learned steering are physically principled and commercially practiced but not yet measured on your acreage; seismic-model representations as inputs to any of these have no published result yet — that is the frontier we are building toward, and it is scored, never assumed. The CO2 flood is a separate discipline (sweep, time-lapse monitoring) and inherits none of these results.

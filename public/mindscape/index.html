The argument, from first principles

Seven things physics already told you about your body.

01second law

Life is a steady state, not a resting state.

There are two ways to hold a value steady. One is thermodynamic equilibrium — the dead kind, where nothing flows because everything has already run down. The other is a steady state: a value held constant precisely because large flows run through it and stay matched — free energy in, entropy out. A living body is entirely the second kind. Its stability is a rate of turnover, not an absence of change.

That's why "balance" is the wrong picture and regulation is the right one. The question is never whether you're in balance; it's which steady state you're holding, and whether it's the one you chose — a faster half marathon, a gut that behaves, steadier energy through the day, a body composition you're after. StatsKey exists to make that steady state visible and steerable, with evidence.

Homeostasis isn't rest. It's a balance of flows, actively held.
02first law

Bookkeeping is physics.

Energy balance isn't diet culture; it's the first law doing accounting the universe enforces whether or not you watch. StatsKey keeps the ledger visible — consumed, burned, basal, active, surplus — and records a meal in seconds by photo, text, barcode, or label, because an instrument you won't use has no resolving power at all.

04ergodicity

You are not the ensemble average.

Population guidance is an ensemble statement — true averaged over many people, silent about any one of them. No theorem promises the ensemble mean describes you. The same meal lands differently in different bodies — energy, digestion, sleep, glucose if you measure it — which is why StatsKey's premise is measure the response, not just the intake: n-of-1 instrumentation instead of borrowed averages.

Every field became a science when measurement replaced introspection — and the strongest constraints come from combining datasets. Cosmology needs the CMB, supernovae, and lensing together; a human needs meals, training, sleep, and gut signals on one timeline — plus glucose, the day you decide a sensor sounds interesting. Today those live in five apps that have never met. StatsKey is the joint fit: one private, connected model of you, with 50+ nutrients per meal and workout import built in.

07observables

The disciplined user is a quantum mechanic.

The deepest lesson measurement theory ever taught: the observables you choose determine what can be known at all, and the question you never measure has no answer you can use. That principle survives the trip to everyday scale intact. So the discipline is choosing your observables — meals, splits, sleep, the gut — and running the measurements every day, the way a mechanic services a machine on a schedule rather than waiting for the check-engine light. Each record updates the best available description of the system. Discipline isn't willpower. It's a high sampling rate.

Pick your observables. Keep the sampling rate honest. Service the only machine you were issued.

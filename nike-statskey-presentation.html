Problem

Health data is everywhere. Meaningful context is missing.

A serious runner already generates miles, heart rate, sleep, routes, and recovery signals every week. Almost nobody is building the harder dataset: comprehensive nutrition, GI patterns, glucose response, and recovery context tied to those runs. That is the missing layer.

Fragmented records

Wearables and workouts are tracked. Comprehensive nutrition, GI patterns, glucose response, and recovery context are rarely connected to them.

Inflated subscriptions

Consumers pay premium prices for apps with increasingly commoditized software.

Unanswered "why"

Users are left guessing what changed, what mattered, and what to try next.

Open plainly: the problem is not that people lack data; it is that the data is fragmented, overpriced, and disconnected from decisions.
Why this matters now

The signals that matter most are the ones people ignore.

Diet, metabolic disease, and digestive health drive a staggering share of preventable U.S. mortality and quality of life — and most people are not tracking any of it.

Runner health signal

GI distress is more common than people admit.

Endurance runners routinely manage cramps, urgency, nausea, bloating, fueling tolerance, and bathroom risk. The same signal generalizes beyond running: digestive health affects tens of millions of Americans, but stigma keeps it out of the data.

01Performance: GI comfort changes fueling, pacing, confidence, and long-run execution.
02Structure: log Bristol type, urgency, timing, food records, hydration, sleep, and training load.
03Generalize: the same framework helps non-runners connect diet, digestion, energy, and recovery.
